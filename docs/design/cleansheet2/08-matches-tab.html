The real table, one tap away

People check the table on the same trip as the scores, so it lives behind a segmented control rather than a fourth bottom tab. No Cashford numbers here — this is the competition as it stands, plainly: played, goal difference, points, last-few form. The same component powers the league Table tab in 02.

Cases & flows

Your standing, collapsed
  • One card, one row per league. League name, your place as an ordinal, your money signed. Never "P1" — 1st of 7, because the field size is half the story.
  • Points sit in the header when they can. Same picks in every league means one points number for the card. The moment a pick differs, the header drops it and every row carries its own — nothing is ever averaged across rooms.
  • One provisional footer, not one per row. Everything in the card is live until the gameweek settles, so it's said once at the bottom.
  • A league you sat out still gets a row, reading "You're not in GW3" with a dash for money. Leaving it out would look like the league had gone.
Results and calls
  • Same call everywhere: one line — the scoreline, the points chip, the verdict.
  • Different calls: the row says "You called it two ways" and opens. Best call first, league named, so you can see which room you got right rather than counting.
  • Void fixture: dashed, no score, no points, and it says when you'll call it again.
  • Not yet played: your call shows with a dash where points will land.
Table
  • Plain competition data. No predictions, no pot, no ranks of your own — mixing the two makes both harder to read.
  • Compressed, not truncated. The top of the table and the bottom of it, with a labelled gap between, because those are the two ends people look at.
  • Form shows only matches played. Two dots after two games, not five dots with three blanks.
  • Source and freshness are stated once, at the top: ESPN, ten minutes during matches, hourly otherwise.
